Fertigation Systems

Applying fertilizer through water is easy. Applying the right fertilizer, at the right concentration, without precipitation and without clogging the system is an engineering task.

Who This Service Is For
  • Commercial growers who will add fertilization to their irrigation system
  • Greenhouse and soilless farming operations (open or closed feeding systems)
  • Those experiencing clogging, precipitation or uneven distribution issues in their existing fertilization unit
  • Operations that want to control fertilizer consumption and cost
Why It Is a Separate Engineering Subject

A fertilization unit added later works — but it works by shortening the life of the system.

"The most common setup we see in the field is this: the irrigation system is installed, months later someone says 'let's add fertilizer too', and a venturi is added to the main line. At that point the job has already become harder. Because fertilization is not an attachment fitted later onto the irrigation system, but an input to the design: which fertilizers will be used affects filter selection, fertilizer groups determine the number of tanks, the number of tanks determines the dosing equipment, and acid use determines the pipe and gasket material. A fertilization unit added later works — but it works by shortening the life of the system."

The Logic of a Properly Installed Unit

The four fundamental design parameters of fertigation

Tank Plan
Tank A
Calcium nitrate and calcium-containing compounds
Tank B
Phosphate and sulfate-containing fertilizers
Acid Tank
pH adjustment, kept separate

1 — Fertilizer compatibility and tank separation

This is the first rule of fertigation and the most frequently violated one: calcium-containing fertilizers cannot be in the same concentrate tank as sulfate- and phosphate-containing fertilizers. Precipitation occurs in the concentrate solution; the precipitate accumulates at the bottom of the tank, fills the filter and clogs the emitter.

2 — pH and lime

In many regions of Turkey, irrigation water is hard (limey). This creates two problems: precipitation inside the emitter and reduced availability of nutrient elements. The solution is the controlled neutralization of the water's lime load with acid.

Acid selection is not merely a matter of pH; every acid is also a nutrient source. Nitric acid adds nitrogen, phosphoric acid adds phosphorus. Therefore, acid selection is made within the fertilizer program.

3 — EC — the measure of nutrient concentration

Electrical conductivity (EC) is the indicator of the total amount of dissolved salts in the solution and is the main control parameter of fertigation.

The critical point is this: the EC of the nutrient solution is the sum of the irrigation water's own EC and the fertilizer contribution. If your water is already salty, your fertilizer program is added on top of that. A program that does not take this into account puts the plant into salt stress.

4 — Injection method

Venturi

Draws in fertilizer by making use of the pressure difference in the main line. Simple, cheap, no moving parts. On the other hand, it creates pressure loss and the injection rate varies according to system pressure. Sufficient for single-channel, simple field applications.

Hydraulic Dosing Pump

Works with the water's own energy, requires no electricity. Performs injection proportional to the flow rate — the concentration remains constant even if the water flow changes. A powerful solution in sites without electricity.

Electric Proportional Dosing Pump

The highest accuracy and control. Required for multi-channel programs, EC/pH closed-loop control and automation integration. The standard in greenhouse and soilless farming.

Tank (Bypass) Injection

The fertilizer tank slowly empties by bypassing the line. The concentration changes over time and cannot be controlled. Not recommended in a modern system.

Safety

The fertigation unit is installed in a way that prevents fertilized water from flowing back into the water source. A check valve, vacuum breaker and, in well systems, an appropriate backflow prevention device are not optional but mandatory.

Greenhouse and Soilless Farming

The area where the margin of error is narrowest

In soilless production, fertigation is irrigation itself: all the nutrients the plant takes in come from the solution. This is the area with the narrowest margin of error.

The academic work of our founding team is directly in this field — a master's thesis on the effect of salinity on plant response in open and closed feeding systems, and doctoral research on irrigation management with root zone moisture sensors in soilless farming.

  • Growing medium selection and water retention behavior (perlite, cocopeat, rockwool)
  • Decision between open (drainage discarded) and closed (drainage recovered) systems
  • Drainage rate target and monitoring of drainage EC
  • Solution disinfection and maintenance of nutrient balance in a closed system
  • Managing irrigation frequency through root zone moisture tracking
Is There a Problem With Your Existing Unit?

The following symptoms point to a setup error:

  • → White or gel-consistency deposits at the bottom of the tank
  • → Filters filling up quickly after fertilization
  • → Lime or biofilm clogging in the emitters
  • → Different plant growth at different points in the field
  • → Fertilizer consumption being higher than expected
  • → Inconsistency between leaf analysis and the fertilizer applied

All of these are situations that can be resolved by correcting the setup without replacing the unit. We can evaluate your system on site and identify the source of the problem.
