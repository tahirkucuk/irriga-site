Rehabilitation & Maintenance

Most irrigation systems lose yield not because they break down, but because they are not maintained. And most can be recovered without installing a new one.

Who This Service Is For

The question to ask before installing a new system

  • → Producers whose system has been running for several years but feel it is no longer performing as it once did
  • → Operations where plant development is lagging in part of the field
  • → Those experiencing dripper clogging, filter problems, or pressure drops
  • → New operators who took over a system and do not know how it was originally designed
  • → Those who want to know whether the existing system can be saved before making a new investment
Silent Loss

Because the system keeps running, the problem cannot be measured. And because it is not measured, it goes unnoticed.

An irrigation system rarely collapses. It degrades slowly. Some of the drippers become partially clogged, but not all. When you look, water is still flowing. One end of the field gets less water, but the plant doesn't die immediately — it just produces less.

A system's true performance comes down to one thing: uniformity — whether equal amounts of water reach every point in the field. Until this is measured, it is unknown how much water the system wastes and how much yield it costs.

Measuring this is half a day's work.

System Audit

Rehabilitation always starts with measurement

Intervening without measuring is, most of the time, spending money in the wrong place.

1. Field flow rate measurement

Actual flow rate at drippers selected from the beginning, middle, and end of the line; uniformity value is calculated.

2. Pressure profile

From the control unit outlet to the end of the line; a faster-than-expected drop indicates clogging or a pipe diameter error.

3. Filtration condition

The suitability of the filter type and mesh opening for the dripper is checked.

4. Identification of clogging type

Lime/calcium → acid flush · iron/manganese → acid + source-side prevention · biofilm/algae → chlorine · physical particles → filtration revision · fertilizer precipitate → fertigation correction · root intrusion (SDI) → chemical + design intervention

5. Hydraulic recalculation

The existing system is recalculated for the current cropping pattern.

At the end of the audit we deliver a written report: the measured uniformity value and its interpretation, identified problems and their causes, prioritised intervention recommendations. And a clear recommendation: rehabilitation or replacement?

Typical Findings

The real cause behind the complaint you hear

"The end of the field gets too little water."

Usually not clogging, but a zone sizing error.

"The drippers keep clogging."

The filter mesh opening is not compatible with the dripper, or a water analysis has never been done.

"The filter fills too frequently."

This is a sign the filter is doing its job — the real question is what the source of that load is.

"The upper part of a sloped field gets too little water."

Non-pressure-compensating drippers have been used. A per-zone pressure regulator is usually sufficient.

"The filter clogs immediately when fertilizer is applied."

Incompatible fertilizers are mixed in concentrated tanks. The solution is to correct the tank configuration, not to replace equipment.

"The system was installed but nobody uses it."

Automation was commissioned but the user was not trained. The easiest problem to solve and the most common one.

Maintenance Program

So the system does not end up in the same place again after rehabilitation

Even if we don't do the maintenance ourselves, we leave the program behind. This is a requirement of the system, not of the service.

Pre-Season

Line flushing, filter maintenance, pressure check, valve and automation test. System ready before the season starts.

In-Season

Filter differential pressure monitoring, regular line-end flushing, visual clogging checks.

End of Season

System drainage, chemical flush, winterisation.

Once a year: Field flow measurement and uniformity check — proves whether the system is still performing as designed.
