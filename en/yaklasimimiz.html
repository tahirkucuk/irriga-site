How is an irrigation system engineered?

Six phases. None are skipped.

Engineering Process

Every project goes through these six steps

From site survey to monitoring, each phase has defined inputs, outputs and checkpoints.

Step 1

Site Survey & Data Collection

Determining water source, soil structure, topography and crop water demand. No filter type is selected without a water analysis; no pipe diameter can be calculated without a topographic survey.

Step 2

Calculation

Crop water consumption (ET), peak flow, zone count, pipe diameter and pump selection calculations. Every item is documented — engineering output, not estimation.

Step 3

Design

Scaled layout plan, control unit schematic, fertigation plan and material take-off. Materials are not ordered until the drawing is approved.

Step 4

Installation

Material acceptance, line routing inspection, control unit assembly and pressure testing. Each phase is tied to an inspection record.

Step 5

Commissioning

Line flushing, flow measurement, comparison with calculations and user training. The system is not handed over until it meets design values.

Step 6

Monitoring & Maintenance

Pre-season inspection, periodic in-season supervision and annual flow measurement. Yield loss is most often caused by lack of maintenance.

Deliverables

Every phase has an output

Written documentation, not verbal commitments. The project file belongs to you.

  • ✓ Site survey report — Water analysis, soil notes, topography summary
  • ✓ Design Basis Summary — Calculation assumptions and selection rationale
  • ✓ Project file — Scaled drawing, schematic, take-off and material list
  • ✓ Inspection records — Installation phase supervision logs
  • ✓ Commissioning report — Flow measurement results and comparison table
  • ✓ Maintenance schedule — Seasonal inspection calendar and user manual
