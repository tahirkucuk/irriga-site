Subsurface Drip Irrigation: A Complete Guide to a System 3× More Efficient Than Surface

📅 28 September 2026 ⏱️ 8 min read ✍️ Irriga Engineering
📌 Table of Contents

Subsurface drip irrigation is exactly what it sounds like: a system where the drip irrigation tubing is laid beneath the soil surface rather than on top. So why is everyone talking about it? Because Turkey is on the brink of a severe drought period and delivering water directly to the plant's root zone — without any evaporation loss — is no longer a luxury; it's a necessity. Experts are pointing to 2040 as a critical threshold: a severe drought that could even trigger mass migration is on the horizon. If you build the right system today, you protect both your water and your money. In this article, we'll cover everything from flow rate, pressure, and burial depth to government grants — all with concrete numbers.

What Is Subsurface Drip Irrigation and Why Now?

In conventional drip irrigation, the tubing sits on the soil surface. What happens on hot days? Water evaporates before it ever reaches the roots. A field project carried out in Gaziantep identified exactly this problem: due to the region's climate, a significant portion of the water was lost to evaporation during irrigation.

The solution was simple but highly effective. The tubing was laid approximately 60 centimetres underground, reaching down to the capillary root zone of the trees. The result? Close to 60% water savings compared to the surface system. Every drop of water delivered goes exactly where the plant needs it — nothing is wasted, and weeds get none of it either.

This technology is only just beginning to spread in Turkey. It is currently being applied in vineyard areas of the Aegean region, industrial tomato fields in the GAP area, and in potato and onion production. So this is not a distant technology for you — it may already be working in a neighbouring field.

Subsurface Drip System Technical Specifications

Whether a system will work or not comes down to three numbers: flow rate, pressure, and burial depth. If you don't get these right, even the most expensive equipment will disappoint.

Flow rate (L/hour): Drip tubing on the market typically has a capacity of 2–4 litres per hour. But the real secret lies in pressure-compensating (PC) emitters. These emitters deliver a constant flow rate even at pressures ranging from 1.0 to 3.5 atm — meaning every plant receives the same 2.0 or 1.6 L/hour regardless of whether it is at the start or end of the lateral line.

Pressure (bar/atm): The ideal pressure range in a drip system is 0.5–1.5 atm. But what if your land is sloped? That's where you need to pay close attention. If you irrigate a sloped field with a single main pipe, pressure that is 1 atm at the top can rise to 3 atm at the bottom. At that point, the emitters don't drip — they spray, and the system loses all its purpose. The solution is clear: always use PC pressure-compensating lateral tubing on sloped land.

Burial depth: How deep you bury the tubing depends on the root structure of the plant. The range is quite wide: from 2 cm to 70 cm.

Burial DepthSuitable Crops
5–15 cmCarrot, onion, potato, spinach, strawberry, garlic, lettuce, industrial tomato and annual vegetables
25 cmTomato, aubergine, pepper, bean, cauliflower, cucumber, sugar beet and legumes
30–40 cmCotton, maize, forage crops (alfalfa), watermelon, melon, tobacco, soya, grape, olive and fruit trees
💡 Expert Tip:

When determining the depth, don't fall into the "deeper is better" trap. If you bury a shallow-rooted lettuce at 40 cm, the water never reaches the roots at all. First get a soil analysis, know the root depth, then position the tubing accordingly. The wrong depth cancels out every advantage of the system.

Drip Irrigation Efficiency: What Do the Numbers Say?

"3× more efficient than surface" sounds like a bold claim, I know. But scientific trials back it up. Look at the figures:

  • Water conveyance efficiency: Properly designed subsurface systems deliver water at up to 95% efficiency. Conventional methods stay in the 60–85% range.
  • Water use efficiency: In a date palm trial, surface drip irrigation yielded 0.96 kg/m³ while subsurface drip irrigation reached 1.32 kg/m³ — a 27% increase.
  • Cotton yield: In a subsurface system, seed cotton yield increased by 21.1% to 3,305 kg/ha at 100% ETc level compared to surface irrigation (2,728 kg/ha).
  • Maize–wheat system: Grain yield was 15.8% higher for maize and 5.2% higher for wheat compared to conventional furrow/flood irrigation, with approximately 55% water savings.

These numbers are no coincidence. When you deliver water directly to the roots without evaporation or percolation losses, the plant experiences no stress, and you get more produce from every cubic metre of water.

Sub-Surface Irrigation Technology: 2026 Government Grants

Now we come to the part everyone wants to know about: cost and grants. The initial investment cost of the system is somewhat higher than conventional drip irrigation, that's true. But the government is seriously supporting this transition.

Under the 2026 Rural Development Investment Programme announced by Minister of Agriculture and Forestry Ibrahim Yumaklı, a total of 12.5 billion lira in grants will be provided. Of this, 11.3 billion lira will be distributed across 3,180 projects. Most importantly: a 1.2 billion lira grant earmarked specifically for water-efficient agricultural irrigation systems will be awarded to 2,047 projects.

So as a farmer looking to switch to subsurface drip irrigation, you can cover a significant portion of your investment through grants. Don't miss this opportunity — quotas are limited and applications are filling up fast.

💡 Expert Tip:

Before applying for a grant, make sure to have a professional technical project prepared. The Ministry screens out projects that are not correctly designed from a technical standpoint. A project with properly documented flow rate calculations, pressure balance, and burial depth will both increase your chances of receiving the grant and prevent problems in the field.

Installation and Maintenance: Key Points to Watch

The greatest advantage of a subsurface system is savings in labour and energy. It largely eliminates disadvantages such as rough terrain, harsh weather conditions, and inadequate water sources that make irrigation difficult. But there are a few critical points to bear in mind during installation and operation:

  • Filtration is essential: Cleaning underground emitters is much harder than surface ones. This makes proper filtration of water before it enters the system absolutely vital. Clogging is the number-one enemy of a buried system.
  • Root intrusion prevention: Plant roots can grow towards the emitters. You need to either choose root-barrier emitters or plan for periodic acid flushing treatments.
  • Pressure control: Especially on sloped land, use PC emitters; otherwise one end of the system will be starved of water.
  • Regular monitoring: Because the system is underground, you can't see problems with the naked eye. Track the system's health by periodically measuring flow rate and pressure values.

A properly installed and maintained system will serve you for 15–20 years. This is not an expense — it is a long-term investment.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is a subsurface drip irrigation system installed?
First, a soil analysis is carried out and the root depth is determined. The appropriate burial depth for the crop (5–70 cm) is selected, PC pressure-compensating lateral tubing is laid, and a high-quality filtration system is installed. Professional technical design is essential for correct flow rate (2–4 L/hour) and pressure (0.5–1.5 atm) settings.
Is subsurface drip irrigation really more efficient?
Yes. Scientific trials show that subsurface systems deliver water at up to 95% efficiency. Water use efficiency improvements of up to 27% in date palms and up to 21% in cotton yields have been recorded. In the Gaziantep case study, water savings of close to 60% were achieved compared to a surface system.
How many times more efficient is subsurface drip irrigation than surface?
In terms of water conveyance efficiency, conventional methods reach around 60% while subsurface systems reach 95%. With evaporation and weed-loss eliminated, the practical difference in effective water use can amount to up to 3× greater efficiency compared to surface irrigation.
How much does a subsurface drip irrigation system cost to install?
Costs vary according to field size, crop type, and equipment selected. However, under the 2026 Rural Development Investment Programme, a 1.2 billion lira grant support is available for water-efficient irrigation systems, meaning the government can cover a significant portion of your investment.
