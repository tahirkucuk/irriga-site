Irrigation System Maintenance: Pre- and Post-Season

📅 15 March 2026 ⏱️ 5 min read ✍️ Irriga Engineering
📌 What's in This Guide?
  1. 1. Why Is Maintenance So Important?
  2. 2. Season Opening Maintenance (Spring)
  3. 3. In-Season Periodic Checks
  4. 4. Season Closing Maintenance (Autumn)
  5. 5. Quick Checklist

An irrigation system is one of the most critical infrastructure investments in agricultural production. A correctly installed system can operate without problems for 10–15 years — but only when regularly maintained. In poorly maintained systems, filter blockages, emitter failures and pipe damage cause unexpected shutdowns and crop losses at the start of the season. This guide summarises the steps to follow to prepare your system for both the season opening and closing.

1. Why Is Maintenance So Important?

A blocked emitter leaves the root zone of that area without water. Dirty filters destabilise system pressure and can cause both overwatering and underwatering simultaneously. Moreover, a small pipe leak can continue unnoticed for months, increasing both water and electricity bills.

📊 Cost Comparison:

Annual maintenance cost is generally around 1–3% of the system's value. Emergency repairs and crop losses in mid-season due to lack of maintenance can reach 5–10 times this figure.

2. Season Opening Maintenance (Spring)

Carry out the following checks 1–2 weeks before the first irrigation:

Pump and Motor Check

  • Check the motor oil and replace it if necessary.
  • Inspect the pump seals (gaskets) for leaks.
  • Review electrical connections and contactors.
  • Run the pump and compare pressure and flow values with last year; if there is a noticeable drop, inspect the impeller or seals.

Filtration System

  • Remove and clean disc or screen filters; inspect for holes and replace any damaged ones.
  • Test whether the hydrocyclone flushing valve is operating.
  • If there is a sand-gravel filter, perform backwashing.

Main Line and Lateral Pipes

  • Inspect sections that may have cracked from frost during winter.
  • Check connection fittings and gaskets.
  • Run the system with the ends of the lateral pipes open; allow sediment and algae accumulated over several minutes to be expelled, then fit the caps.

Emitter Inspection

Run the system and check whether each emitter is working properly. Blocked emitters must be replaced; those with very high or very low pressure should be investigated. Lime scale build-up is the leading cause of emitter problems especially in systems using fertigation.

Valve and Control Systems

  • Test solenoid valves in both manual and automatic mode.
  • Update the settings of the control unit or automation panel according to the season programme.
  • Check sensor calibrations.

3. In-Season Periodic Checks

Apply the following routine checks on weekly and monthly cycles:

  • Weekly: Read the filter pressure differential (with a pressure gauge); if the difference exceeds 0.3 bar, it is time to clean the filter.
  • Weekly: Check the flow at the end of a few random lateral lines; monitor for signs of blockage or leakage.
  • Monthly: Run all zones and observe water distribution; investigate dry or excessively wet spots.
  • Monthly: Clean the fertiliser tank, dosing pump and injector head in the fertigation system.

4. Season Closing Maintenance (Autumn)

Preparing the system for winter dormancy after harvest is the key to a trouble-free opening next season.

System Flushing

Before shutdown, open all lateral pipes to expel accumulated sediment, algae and organic matter from the system. A short acid flush with filter-cleaning acid (citric acid solution) dissolves lime scale deposits.

Winter Drainage

In frost-prone areas, completely drain the lateral pipes and main line. PVC pipe is at risk of cracking below 0°C. Blowing through the system with a compressed-air compressor is an effective method for expelling remaining water.

Pump and Motor

  • Run the pump to drain the water inside it.
  • Clean the motor cover and check that ventilation openings are clear.
  • For fuel-powered motors, clean the carburettor and drain the fuel tank.

Equipment Storage

  • Clean removable filters and store them in a dry environment.
  • Keep control units away from moisture.
  • List emitters that have broken or worn out during the year and obtain spares over winter.

5. Quick Checklist

✅ Before Season Opening:

☐ Pump pressure and flow test  |  ☐ Filter cleaning  |  ☐ Lateral flushing  |  ☐ Emitter inspection  |  ☐ Valve test  |  ☐ Automation programme update

✅ After Season Closing:

☐ System flushed  |  ☐ Pipes drained  |  ☐ Filters cleaned & stored  |  ☐ Pump winterised  |  ☐ Spare parts list prepared

A systematic maintenance programme both extends equipment life and enables you to manage the irrigation season from start to finish without problems. Whenever you need technical maintenance support, Irriga engineers are ready to assist on-site or remotely.
