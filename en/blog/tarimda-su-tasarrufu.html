How to Achieve 30% Water Savings in Agriculture?

📅 20 July 2026 ⏱️ 5 min read ✍️ Irriga Engineering
📌 What's in This Guide?

  2. 2. Correct System Selection

Turkey is a country where agricultural water consumption accounts for 72% of total fresh water use. With declining rainfall and increasing evaporation driven by global warming, irrigation efficiency has become a critical competitive advantage for farmers. The good news is: with current technology and correct practices, achieving 25–45% water savings is a completely accessible goal today.

1. Why Is Agricultural Water Efficiency Important?

In traditional furrow irrigation systems, only 45–55% of the water reaches the plant; the rest evaporates, is lost to surface runoff or seeps to depths beyond the root zone where plants cannot benefit from it. This waste creates a heavy burden not only financially but also in terms of irrigation quotas and licence costs in water-scarce areas.

📊 The Numbers:

According to DSI data, irrigation water efficiency in Turkey averages 45%. With modern drip irrigation systems, this rate rises above 90%.

2. Correct System Selection

The first and most critical step in water saving is selecting the irrigation system that suits the crop type and land structure.

Drip Irrigation

It is the most efficient method for orchards, vegetable fields and greenhouse production. Water is delivered directly to the root zone; surface wetting and evaporation are minimised. A well-designed drip irrigation system provides 40–60% water savings compared to surface irrigation.

Sprinkler Irrigation

While suitable for cereals, fodder crops and large plots, evaporation losses are high in non-modernised sprinkler systems. Losses can be significantly reduced with low-pressure rotator heads and night-time irrigation scheduling.

Correct Equipment Sizing

Correctly sizing pump power, pipe diameters and emitter flow rates through hydraulic calculations directly affects water and energy efficiency throughout the system's service life. Selecting an oversized pump wastes energy, while undersizing leads to insufficient irrigation and crop loss.

3. Irrigation Scheduling

No matter how good the system is, incorrect timing and quantities make water saving impossible.

Early Morning Irrigation

Irrigating between 05:00–09:00 reduces evaporation loss by 20–30% compared to midday. Wind effect is also minimal, improving distribution uniformity in sprinkler systems.

Programme According to Plant Growth Stage

The water requirements of plants differ at each stage — germination, seedling, growth and maturation. Dynamic scheduling that follows the growth phase rather than a fixed programme makes water use 15–25% more efficient.

Weather Forecast Integration

Weather station integrations that automatically stop irrigation on days when rainfall is forecast can save 10–20% of annual irrigation water.

4. Sensor Integration

Soil moisture sensors are the most powerful water-saving tool in modern irrigation systems.

Soil Moisture Sensors

Capacitive or TDR sensors placed in the root zone measure the actual moisture content of the soil. Irrigation starts when the reading falls below a set threshold and stops automatically once the threshold is met. This method provides 25–35% additional water savings.

Remote Monitoring Platforms

Cloud-based platforms such as WiseConn DropControl transmit sensor data to a mobile app in real time. The farmer can optimise irrigation duration and flow rate in real time from their phone. Going to the field to open a valve is no longer necessary.

💡 Irriga Case Study:

The Smart Soil moisture sensor + WiseConn automation integration installed on a 120-decare vineyard in Manisa Alaşehir enabled the producer to reduce irrigation water by 34% compared to the previous season. Grape yield increased by 18% in the same period.

5. Regular Maintenance and Monitoring

Even the best-designed system turns into water waste when left without maintenance.

  • Filter Cleaning: Blocked filters disrupt system pressure, causing overwatering or underwatering. Checks at the start and middle of the season are essential.
  • Emitter Inspection: Blocked emitters cause root stress, while blocked lateral pipes cause excessive pressure. Monthly walk-through checks or pressure-reading sensors should be used for monitoring.
  • Line Leak Detection: Underground pipe leaks cause both water waste and moisture accumulation outside the root zone. In modern systems, flow measurement sensors instantly alert to sudden flow increases.

In projects where all these steps are systematically implemented, achieving total water savings of 30% to 50% is a realistic target. Every cubic metre of water saved translates directly into profit from reduced energy bills and irrigation quota costs.
