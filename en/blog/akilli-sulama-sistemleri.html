Smart Irrigation: Sensor and Remote Control Systems

📅 15 April 2026 ⏱️ 5 min read ✍️ Irriga Engineering
📌 What's in This Guide?

Digital transformation in agriculture is no longer confined to large-scale operations. Thanks to affordable sensors, cloud platforms and powerful mobile apps, a family farm of just 10 acres can manage its irrigation remotely and data-driven. In this guide we explain the components of smart irrigation and how they are used in practice.

1. Why Smart Irrigation?

Traditional timer-based irrigation systems operate on a fixed schedule — they have no knowledge of how much rainfall has fallen, what the actual soil moisture content is, or the day's evaporation rate. This blindness leads to water waste as well as crop losses caused by over- or under-irrigation.

Smart irrigation systems, on the other hand, collect real-time data to ensure irrigation is applied exactly when it is needed and in exactly the right amount. The result: 25–40% water savings, 15–30% yield increase and a dramatic reduction in labour.

2. Soil Moisture Sensors

The fundamental input for smart irrigation is soil moisture. Two main technologies are widely used:

Capacitive Sensors

These calculate water content by measuring the capacitance of the electric field passing through the soil. Low-cost, long-lasting and maintenance-free, they are adequate for most field applications. Sentek (the brand distributed by Smart Soil) is the industry standard in this category.

TDR (Time Domain Reflectometry) Sensors

These measure the propagation speed of an electromagnetic wave through the soil; more precise but more expensive. They are preferred in research stations and precision agriculture projects.

Sensor Depth and Placement

A single sensor is not sufficient. In an ideal installation, sensors are placed at 3 different depths (15 cm, 30 cm, 60 cm). The top layer shows germination and surface root development, the middle layer shows the main root zone, and the bottom layer indicates whether water is escaping deep into the soil (percolation).

💡 Practical Tip:

Place sensors at 3 different points in the field: the areas where the soil appears driest, average and most moist. Data read from a single point may not represent the entire field.

3. Weather Station Integration

The second layer that complements soil moisture data is climatic data. Compact weather stations installed at the field edge take the following measurements:

  • Temperature and Relative Humidity: The basic input for estimating transpiration.
  • Solar Radiation: Calculates the crop's water demand based on light intensity (ET calculation).
  • Wind Speed and Direction: Distribution correction for sprinkler systems and evaporation estimation.
  • Rain Gauge: Reports natural rainfall to the system; cancels or shortens scheduled irrigation.

These data are used in the ET (evapotranspiration) calculation to estimate the amount of water the crop has lost that day and automatically adjust irrigation.

4. Remote Control Platforms

WiseConn DropControl

WiseConn DropControl, Irriga's preferred irrigation automation platform, is actively used on over 500,000 acres. RF-based control devices are installed without requiring cables. The cloud-based platform consolidates sensor data, irrigation schedules and alarm notifications on a single screen.

From a smartphone, the user can:

  • Open and close each irrigation zone independently.
  • Monitor real-time flow and pressure values.
  • Receive alarm notifications (line break, excessive pressure, flow anomaly).
  • Download weekly irrigation reports.

Smart Soil Sentek Integration

Smart Soil's Sentek soil moisture sensors can integrate directly with the DropControl platform. When soil moisture drops below a defined lower threshold, the system automatically starts irrigation; when it reaches the upper limit, it stops. This closed-loop control virtually eliminates human intervention.

5. Investment Evaluation

Typical cost ranges for smart irrigation components (2026 market average):

  • Soil Moisture Sensor (3 depths, 1 point): 8,000–25,000 ₺
  • WiseConn RF Control Unit (per zone): 15,000–35,000 ₺
  • Weather Station: 20,000–50,000 ₺
  • Installation and Integration: 10,000–20,000 ₺

The total automation investment is generally between 50,000–130,000 ₺. With labour savings (valve opening/closing, field inspection rounds), water savings and reduced crop losses, this investment is typically recovered within 2–3 seasons.
